Price Comparison Survey

Find the price shoppers accept, before you print it.

Request a Price StudyAll Research and Insights formats

Last updated: 26 November 2026

What it is

A price comparison survey shows shoppers your product and competing products at different price points and records which they would choose. You receive an Excel report with charts that shows how demand shifts as the price moves. It is a practical check before a launch or a price change.

Works best when

  • You are choosing a launch price or planning a price change.
  • A competitor has moved its price and you need to respond.
  • You sell in a price-sensitive category such as FMCG or personal care.
  • You want evidence for an internal pricing decision.

How a typical run goes

  1. Set the options

    We agree the products, the price points to test and the competitors to include. You provide product visuals or samples.

  2. Design the choice

    Respondents see cards or real packs at set prices and choose one, so the question feels like a real shop decision.

  3. Fieldwork

    Surveyors ask people at relevant sites, such as outside supermarkets or in markets, and record choices and reasons.

  4. Report

    We deliver an Excel report with charts, price-by-choice tables and short findings.

What we need from you

  • The price points you want to test.
  • Competitor products and their current prices.
  • Product visuals or sample packs.
  • Target shoppers and locations.

What you receive

  • An Excel report with charts and tables.
  • Choice shares at each price point.
  • Reasons given for choices, grouped by theme.
  • A method note with sample size and limits.

Honest limits

  • Stated choices are not purchases. Real behaviour can differ from what people say.
  • Street samples are practical samples and not nationally representative.
  • Prices tested should be realistic. We advise on the range, and you decide.

Frequently asked questions

Does it predict sales?

No. It shows preference at stated prices, which is a guide, not a forecast.

Can we test more than two prices?

Yes. The more price points, the larger the sample you need. We set this in the scope.

Can you include our competitors?

Yes. You name them, and we show them at their current shelf prices.

Can we repeat it later?

Yes. Repeating the same questions shows how preference has moved.
